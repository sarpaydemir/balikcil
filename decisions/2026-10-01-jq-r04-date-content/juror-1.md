## 0 · The one reading that runs through all six parts

All six parts set RULES 9 against the TACTICS 3 list. I answer them all from one
reading of three texts. Where a part depends on another, I say so under that part.

1. **What TACTICS 6 requires is the floor.** RULES 9 (`RULES.md` line 41: "In the
   exam the coin name and the date are hidden.") is carried out by the list in
   `TACTICS.md` lines 101–107. Those items must be hidden. Nothing outside that
   list is *required* to be hidden by those two texts, unless the item is the
   date, the time or the coin name itself in another printed place.
2. **The exam card is the card with things taken away, never added.** `README.md`
   lines 41–42: "Cards from the exam coins are shown, but only the part before the
   movement. The coin name and date are hidden." `TACTICS.md` lines 99–100: "Nadia
   has 400 cards prepared from the exam coins … The cards contain only the
   'before' section." `TEAM.md` lines 68–69: Nadia "Strips the names and dates,
   hides the price, seals the answer key." Every verb here (shown only in part,
   hidden, strips, hides) takes something away. None of them adds information the
   card (TACTICS 3) does not carry. The only conversions the text itself names are
   in TACTICS 6 lines 104 and 106.
3. **Hiding more than the floor is permitted only for a stated reason.** The
   question's case is a measured coin signature on a field the frozen book does not
   read. Without such a reason, a field TACTICS 3 lists stays.

The DATE parts ask what the texts **require** ("Does … require removing",
"Does hiding … cover"). CONTENT-a asks what the blinding **may** do. A "no" on the
DATE parts and a "yes" on CONTENT-a do not contradict each other. Something can be
permitted without being required. I show where that distinction carries weight.

## JQ-R04-DATE-a · columns that tie cards to the same clock hours

**Answer.** No. RULES 9 and TACTICS 6's "the date and time" do not require removing
the bitcoin and ethereum columns. Those columns print no date and no time, and
TACTICS 3 puts them on the card precisely as "over the same hours".

**What it rests on.**
- `TACTICS.md` line 61 (§3, on the card): "bitcoin and ethereum, over the same
  hours". The field is *defined* by its hours. Any two cards whose windows overlap
  carry identical values here by construction, so the authors put a same-hours
  field on the card.
- `TACTICS.md` line 103 (§6, hidden): "the date and time". The item is the date and
  the time. It is not a list of fields from which a time could be inferred.
  Contrast this with line 107, which reaches *into* a named field ("the date in the
  release calendar"). The authors knew how to name a field. They named no market
  column.
- `TACTICS.md` line 127 and `RULES.md` lines 53–54 (RULES 13): "A moment appearing
  in several cards in the same hour counts as a single event." The laboratory
  expected cards of different coins to share hours. It dealt with that in
  *scoring*, not in hiding.
- What JQ-R04-DATE lines 66–69 measures is **linkage**: two cards "share an hour".
  It is not *which* hours. Lines 71–73: placing a card in time "has not been
  measured". The measured fact is that two cards cover the same hours. That is not
  the date.

**Strongest case against.** RULES 9 exists so that the candidate cannot bring
outside knowledge of what happened at that time. The candidates are language models
that may have read about this period. An hour-by-hour bitcoin path around a famous
crash could be recognised and the card dated. RULES 22 (`RULES.md` lines 76–77:
"'Could not be measured' never turns into 'no problem'") forbids treating the
unmeasured dating risk as zero. A second risk is separate from the date: the linkage
lets a candidate see that several cards share one window. That may hint at a
market-wide move, and so at the label. RULES 13 collapses such cards to one event in
the score, but it does not stop the candidate from seeing the cluster. I name this
as unresolved. It is not a date question, and I do not decide it.

**Confidence 4.** I would change my mind if I saw a measurement that a tool-less
candidate (RULES 10) dates cards from these columns better than chance. That would
show the column functions as the date for the actual reader. I would still say the
remedy is a separate question.

## JQ-R04-DATE-b · release names that identify a calendar day

**Answer.** No. Hiding "the date in the release calendar" covers the date printed
in that field, not the release's name. That holds even for a name that, to a reader
who knows the public calendar, implies the day.

**What it rests on.**
- `TACTICS.md` line 107 (§6): "the date in the release calendar". The authors
  addressed this exact field and chose to hide *the date in* it, not the field and
  not its entries.
- `TACTICS.md` line 62 (§3, on the card): "US release calendar (inflation,
  employment, rate decision)". The calendar's content is named releases. The
  authors' own three examples are publicly scheduled releases, so a reader who
  knows the public calendar can narrow them to a few days a year (general
  knowledge, not a laboratory measurement). The authors still put names on the card
  and hid only the date. A reading that treats a date-implying name as "the date"
  would remove the very entries TACTICS 3 names as the field's content. That is not
  a reading of TACTICS 6. It is a rewrite of TACTICS 3.
- JQ-R04-DATE lines 105–108: the "13 names" figure measures appearance on one day
  "within these 306 cards — not how often the release is published". Lines 111–112:
  whether a tool-less candidate can date a card from a name "has not been
  measured". Nothing measured shows that the names function as the date.

**Strongest case against.** RULES 9 outranks TACTICS (`RULES.md` line 3: "These
rules do not change."). If a name *by itself* lets a reader fix the calendar date,
then in substance the date is not hidden, whatever TACTICS 6's wording. An annual
release inside a one-year period is close to such a case. TACTICS 6 also already
reaches past literal printing on the coin side ("the coin name inside
announcements", line 105). "The date in the release calendar" could be the same
move for the date: wherever the release calendar betrays the date, hide it. A juror
who reads the item by its purpose rather than its object would answer yes, and that
is a respectable reading.

**Confidence 3.** I would change my mind if a ratified verdict or a laboratory text
read TACTICS 6's date items by their purpose, or if it were measured that a
tool-less candidate dates cards from release names. A separate point, which is not
mine: whether obscure releases such as the 13 listed belong in a calendar TACTICS 3
describes as "(inflation, employment, rate decision)" is a scope question about
TACTICS 3. It was not asked, and I do not answer it.

## JQ-R04-DATE-c · hour offsets to releases with a public clock time

**Answer.** No. "The date and time" covers a printed date or clock time. It does not
cover the time of day a reader could work out by combining a printed relative offset
with outside knowledge of when a release is published.

**What it rests on.**
- `TACTICS.md` line 103: "the date and time". This is what is printed.
- `TACTICS.md` line 50 (§3): "the 24 hours before the start, hour by hour". The
  card is relative to its own start by design. An offset in hours is the same kind
  of information as a row label `h-7`.
- `TACTICS.md` line 107 hides "the date in the release calendar". It does not hide
  the offset, and it does not hide the release's hour relative to the card.
- `TACTICS.md` line 62 again. "Rate decision" and the other named kinds are released
  at publicly known clock times (general knowledge, not measured here). The authors
  put them on a card whose rows are hours. The same inference was open to the
  authors and was not closed.
- JQ-R04-DATE lines 129–130: how many releases have a fixed public time, and whether
  a candidate knows it, "has not been measured".

**Depends on.** This rests on the same reading as DATE-b (the item hides what is
printed, not what a reader can infer from public knowledge). A juror who answers b
"yes" by purpose should answer c "yes" too. Mine are consistent.

**Strongest case against.** TACTICS 6 lists "time" separately from "date". Time of
day matters to trading, and it is the very thing the item was written to hide. An
offset to a release with a well-known clock time gives the card's start hour
exactly. A language model candidate plausibly knows common US release times, so
"tool-less" does not protect much here. That is plausible, not measured. Once
"time" is hidden in print and recovered by arithmetic, it is hidden in name only.
There is also no stopping point on my side. Other hour-by-hour fields may carry
time-of-day patterns. I have not checked how the card prints them, so I do not rely
on that point either way.

**Confidence 3.** I would change my mind on the same evidence as b. I would also
change it if a laboratory text showed that "time" in TACTICS 6 was meant as "time of
day, however recovered".

## JQ-R04-CONTENT-a · leaving out the trade-count column

**Answer.** Yes, in exactly the case posed and no wider. The blinding *may* leave
out a column TACTICS 3 puts on the card when (i) nothing the frozen canteen book
asks for reads it, and (ii) in the rendering the exam card would otherwise carry,
the column carries a measured coin signature as JQ-R04-CARRIES defines one. This is
a permission, not a requirement. It is not a ground on which columns that fail
either condition may be dropped.

**What it rests on.**
- `TACTICS.md` lines 104 and 106. TACTICS 6 itself reads "the coin name … hidden" as
  reaching identifying *content*, not only the printed name: "the price itself
  (converted to a number starting from 100)" and "the Wikipedia number itself (given
  as a ratio to the coin's own average)". Neither is a name. Both are listed
  because their level identifies the coin. On the coin side, the laboratory's own
  text treats identifying content as within RULES 9. For the date, TACTICS 6 lists
  only printed dates, which is why DATE a–c come out the other way.
- `decisions/2026-10-01-jq-r04-gate/verdict.md` line 72 (ratified, 3–0): "The gate
  fails if either the nearest-neighbour attack or the pair AUC attack beats its own
  RULES 12 chance line on the exam cards." Ratified law already treats a measured
  coin signature on exam cards as a failure. Under a "no", a column that carried one
  and could not be converted would leave the exam with no permitted way through,
  short of a rule change the user has declined to be asked about.
- `TACTICS.md` line 55: "What is on the card (where data exists):". The list does
  not promise that every card carries every field. Line 71: "the card is kept
  short". Lines 99–100 and `README.md` lines 41–42: the exam card is the card with
  parts taken away. Leaving a column out is taking away. It adds nothing.
- The JQ-R04-CARRIES outcome (as quoted in my instruction) fixes what "carries a
  measured coin signature" means. I do not apply it to any figure.
- Condition (i) protects RULES 6 (`RULES.md` lines 34–35: "A rule is not changed
  after looking at a result."). The frozen book's rules are tested exactly as frozen.
  For the trade count, JQ-R04-CONTENT lines 128–137 say no rule, blocker, score or
  exit reads it. B-2's lifting condition (`canteen/2026-09-19-sofia.md` lines
  224–225: "lifts only when somebody checks the column against the 5-minute taker
  archive and states a trade-count floor") is assigned at lines 669–671 to "Mateo/
  Nadia against the 5-minute taker archive, as a data-cleaning decision, not a
  trading decision". That points to the archive, not the printed card. The book does
  not say so outright (JQ-R04-CONTENT lines 136–137). **If** a stated floor were
  ever to be applied to the card's printed trade count, condition (i) would fail and
  the column would have to return. That is reversible while exam cards are unsealed.

**Depends on.** Part c decides which rendering is "the rendering the exam card would
otherwise carry". Under my c ("no"), that rendering is the rank from the printed
values. Part a applies only if *that* rendering carries a measured signature.
CONTENT-b's option b3 depends on this answer.

**Strongest case against.** TACTICS 6 lists, closed in form, what is hidden. Where it
deals with a coin-identifying field it **converts** the field (rebase, ratio). It
never **removes** one. The laboratory's own pattern is therefore "transform, keep".
Removal goes past the pattern. TACTICS 3 line 56 names "trade count" outright. A
blinding that may drop listed fields whenever an audit finds a signature hands
engineering a power over card content that the text gave nobody. Tomás, who reads
everything on common sense, loses a field the observation cards had. A juror could
also say the gate's failure is the laboratory's problem to take to the user, not a
reason to read a permission into TACTICS 3. Finally, a pair AUC or nearest-neighbour
signature measures that cards of one coin can be *linked*, not that the coin can be
*named*. I treat linkage as the laboratory's operational meaning only because the
ratified gate does.

**Confidence 3.** I would change my mind if I saw that the ratified gate is not a
condition the exam must pass, or that the frozen book reads the trade count after
all.

## JQ-R04-CONTENT-b · the price column

**Answer.**
- **b1, permitted.** This is TACTICS 6's own text. The number of decimals is a
  rounding choice under TACTICS 3 line 71 and is engineering, not mine.
- **b2, not permitted as TACTICS 6's price rendering**, unless it prints, token for
  token, what b1 prints at the same decimals, in which case it *is* b1.
- **b3, permitted only through CONTENT-a.** That is, only if no rule in the frozen
  book reads the price column and the rendering the card would otherwise carry (b1)
  carries a measured coin signature as JQ-R04-CARRIES defines one.

**What it rests on.**
- `TACTICS.md` line 104: "the price itself (converted to a number starting from
  100)". This names a source, the price itself, and a conversion, rebasing to 100.
  b1 is exactly that, rounded.
- `TACTICS.md` line 71: "Numbers are rounded and the card is kept short." This
  licenses printing a value *rounded*. It does not license printing a *different*
  value. Each b1 entry is the rebased price rounded, off by at most half a unit in
  its last printed decimal by construction. Each b2 entry is a compounding of other
  rounded numbers. Its difference from the rebased price accumulates over the hours,
  and JQ-R04-CONTENT lines 182–183 say that difference is unmeasured. Printing b2
  under the name of the price would put on the card a number whose relation to what
  it claims to be is unmeasured (`RULES.md` lines 71–72, RULES 19: "An unmeasured
  number is not written down.").
- b3: `TEAM.md` line 69, "hides the price", and the reasoning of CONTENT-a.
  `canteen/2026-09-19-sofia.md` lines 116–118: S-1 reads `chg%`, and only "If an exam
  card does not print `chg%`, compute it from the `close` column". With `chg%`
  printed, S-1 does not need `close`. **I could not check whether any other frozen
  rule reads the price column.** I was allowed only the lines listed. Condition (i)
  of CONTENT-a has to be checked against the whole frozen book by someone allowed to
  read it.

**Depends on.** b3 depends wholly on CONTENT-a. My objection to b2 uses the same
principle as my c answer: the card may round a value and may take information away,
but it may not substitute a different value. Consistency with c raises one point I
name and do not decide. b1 as posed rebases the "actual price". If at the chosen
decimals that showed distinctions the raw card's printed price does not show, my c
reasoning would say the rebase should start from the printed price. Whether that
happens on any card is unmeasured. Part b was not posed that way.

**Strongest case against.** For b2: TACTICS 6 wants the candidate to see the price
*path* from 100. b2 gives that path, adds no information beyond `chg%`, and differs
from b1 only by rounding drift that is plausibly smaller than anything a reader uses.
Demanding token-for-token equality is in effect a threshold of zero, and RULES 33
bars me from setting thresholds. My reply is that zero here is not a tolerance I
chose. It is the identity test: either the printed number is the price rounded or
it is not. But a referee could fairly call it strict. Against b1: the measured table
(JQ-R04-CONTENT lines 162–163) shows b1 carries a signature at both decimal settings
tried. Permitting b1 permits a known leak. My answer is that b1 is the text, and the
leak is what CONTENT-a and the gate deal with.

**Confidence.** b1: 4 · b2: 3 · b3: 3. I would change my mind on b2 if a laboratory
text treated reconstructed values as "rounded" values. I would change it on b3 if
any frozen rule reads `close` when `chg%` is printed.

## JQ-R04-CONTENT-c · where a ranked column's order comes from

**Answer.** No. A ranked column on an exam card must be computed from the values the
card prints. It may not order hours the raw card prints as equal.

**What it rests on.**
- `README.md` lines 41–42 ("Cards from the exam coins are shown, but only the part
  before the movement. The coin name and date are hidden."), `TACTICS.md` lines
  99–100, and `TEAM.md` line 69 ("Strips … hides …"). The exam card is the card
  minus what is hidden. Hiding takes information away. A rank of pre-rounding values
  orders hours that the card, as TACTICS 3 makes it, shows as equal. That *adds*
  information the card does not carry.
- `TACTICS.md` line 71: "Numbers are rounded and the card is kept short." The card
  TACTICS 3 defines is the rounded card, and its information is the rounded values.
- Ranking itself is not among TACTICS 6's named conversions (lines 101–107). It can
  stand only as a hiding device, a conversion that removes the level. A rank of the
  printed values is purely a function of the card and so purely hiding. A rank of
  pre-rounding values is not a function of the card.
- The "yes" option's argument that TACTICS 6 converts "the X itself" does not carry
  it. In lines 104 and 106, "itself" contrasts the absolute level with the
  converted form. It says nothing about rounded against unrounded.

**Depends on.** This fixes the rendering CONTENT-a refers to: the printed-values
rank. If the trade-count column in that rendering carries a measured coin signature
per JQ-R04-CARRIES, CONTENT-a's permission applies. Pre-rounding ranks are not
available as a way to remove a signature.

**Strongest case against.** The rounding in TACTICS 3 exists to keep the card short
and spare the AI raw seconds. It does not exist to withhold order. A rank from 1 to
24 is just as short either way, and no unrounded number is printed. The exam card
already departs from the raw card far more than this: it prints ranks, not values,
which watchers never saw. So the "parity with observation" argument is already
broken by the ranking the laboratory chose. The question also reports, measured,
that the frozen book's visible triggers are preserved under pre-rounding ranks
(B-3 hours, B-4 runs, `chg%` unchanged; JQ-R04-CONTENT lines 214–219). And if
pre-rounding ranks remove a coin signature that printed ranks carry, "yes" keeps a
TACTICS 3 field on the card that my CONTENT-a answer might otherwise let go. That
is arguably more faithful to TACTICS 3 than removal.

**Confidence 3.** I would change my mind if a laboratory text described the exam
card as built from the underlying data rather than from the card. TACTICS 6 line 99,
"prepared from the exam coins", is the nearest such text, and I read it as naming
the coins, not the data layer.

## Reversibility and cost

- Every answer here governs how exam cards are built. **Before the exam cards are
  sealed, every answer is reversible** at the cost of rebuilding and re-auditing the
  cards. That cost is unmeasured, and I give no number.
- **After the exam is sat, nothing is reversible.** A card later found to be
  dateable or coin-identifiable means a compromised paper. The irreversible risk
  sits with my DATE "no" answers: they leave fields on the card whose dating power
  is unmeasured. The question files say the audit will name every measured channel
  in the exam manifest. That reports the risk; it does not remove it.
- CONTENT-a "yes" is the reversible side. A dropped column can be restored before
  sealing.
