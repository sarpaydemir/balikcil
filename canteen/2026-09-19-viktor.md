## 3 · Seven objections that apply to whole families

I state these once and reference them by number. Each is measured, not asserted.

**O1 · The batch is not the set.** Every count in all 36 round-1 files is out of 34 cards.
Round 2 refuted eleven named directional ideas with card-verified counterexamples drawn
from other batches (Amara F1; Ingrid A1–A7; Kenji B6, B8, B9). Ingrid A9 states the
general result: she looked, across all 27 files she read, for a trigger measured in more
than one batch that kept its sign, and **found none**. Consequence: "fires on N cards in
one batch with no counterexample" is not evidence. It is the expected appearance of any
threshold chosen after seeing 34 answers.

**O2 · The base rate is 50/50, not the batch's.** Measured three times independently and
agreeing in all nine batches (Kenji B4, Amara A4, Lukas round-2 §F): **153 large / 153
calm**. Batch rates run 38% (b07) to 62% (b04). Every "lift against the base rate"
computed inside one batch is wrong by up to 12 points.

**O3 · The direction base rate on large cards is 60/40 up.** Kenji B5, from Lukas's own
printed per-card moves: **92 up / 61 down of 153** (note-derived). Every "N of N up" must
be scored against 60%, not 50%. Kenji's worked consequence: 5 of 5 up is what chance
produces about one time in twelve at a 60% base.

**O4 · RULES 13 is violated across the whole set, and it is now measured.**
`data/overlap/overlap-manifest.md`, run `12ce59e2902a0034`: **495 pairs of cards share at
least one clock hour**; **296 of 306 cards** take part in at least one overlap; only **10
cards of 306** share no clock hour with any other card; the largest connected chain is
**20 cards**; the largest single-hour group is **7 cards**; **15 start hours carry more
than one card**. The cross-check compared 12,579 shared hours and found **0** differing
BTC or ETH values, against a negative control in which random *different* hours match on
both columns 0.01% of the time. So the cards are not 306 independent observations, and
**no count in any of the 40 files has been collapsed.** Kenji B1/B2 gives one concrete
instance: 12 of the 153 large cards fall inside eight days of October 2025, spread across
five batches so that no watcher held more than three of them.

**O5 · `calm` does not mean "small", and the two labels overlap in absolute size.**
Card-verified by all four watchers: calm C072 −14.11%, C127 −13.83%, C108 +13.25% against
large C014 −7.35%, C003 +7.72%, C013 +7.91%. Consequence: any idea whose supporting
evidence is "a big move followed" is **not evidence about the label**, and no absolute
percentage threshold can reproduce it — it would sort coins, not moments.

**O6 · The level of every column is a coin fingerprint.** Kenji says it in every batch for
`L/S acct` and `top L/S pos`; Ingrid for funding level and interval; Lukas for depth and
average trade size; Amara for Wikipedia. Consequence: **every trigger written on an
absolute level is a coin selector**, and in an exam that hides the coin name it reads a
hidden label. Kenji B10: Wikipedia is present on exactly the **40 BCH cards of 306** and
on no others, three independent per-batch counts agreeing (8, 5, 0, 2, 4, 8, 5, 5, 3).

**O7 · Three of the five outside-world sources carry nothing.** Announcements `MISSING` on
**306/306** with byte-identical probe text (Ingrid and Amara read all 306 separately;
Kenji spot-checked 12; Lukas confirms). Prediction market: reached and genuinely empty on
306/306. Wikipedia present on 40/306. Consequence: no idea may rest on them — and equally,
**nobody may write "no announcement" about C169 (−51.75%), C168 (+135.21%), C106
(+120.84%), C104 (+161.78%), C243 (+140.96%), C228 (−50.46%) or C107 (−52.36%)**. RULES
21: a technical failure is not a result.
