## Three numbers I derived, so the notes can be checked

All three are arithmetic on numbers printed on the card; they are my derivations, not card
fields. I rounded to 2 decimals.

- **BDR** (bid-depth ratio) = mean(`depth -1%` at h-2 and h-1) ÷ median(`depth -1%`, h-24…h-3).
- **RV** = mean of |`chg%`| over h-24…h-1 (a pure restatement of price; it carries no
  information that price itself does not already carry).
- **LVR** (late volume ratio) = mean(`quote vol`, h-6…h-1) ÷ the card's own stated
  "Previous 7 days · avg hourly volume".

## Ideas (trigger · direction · exit)

**Idea 1 — fragile, fitted on 8 cards, offered only so it can be tested blind.**
Trigger: at the start hour, BDR ≥ 1.20 (mean `depth -1%` over the last two hours ≥ 1.20 ×
the median `depth -1%` over h-24…h-3) **and** the coin's price over the preceding 24 hours
is up ≥ +9%. Direction: sell (short). Exit: at the close 24 hours after entry, or on a
+10% adverse move, whichever comes first. Support in this batch: C153 (-21.00%), C139
(-39.29%), C221 (-38.18%) — 3 cards, all three fell; no calm card in the batch triggers it.
Three cards is not a rule; I expect this to fail the blind exam and say so in advance · 2

## Where I looked and found nothing

- I looked for a level or a trend in `depth -1%`/`depth +1%` that separates the groups
  without normalising by the coin's own recent median: none — the levels differ by coin
  (from ~6k on C153/C163 to ~2.4M on C033) far more than by label.
- I looked at trade count on its own (and at quote volume ÷ trades, the average trade
  size). It moves by an order of magnitude inside single cards — C131 h-17 has 872.43k on
  106k trades while h-22 has 870.95k on 14k trades; C102, C120 and C106 show the same kind
  of jump — so I could not use it to separate anything, and I do not claim it is empty of
  information, only that I could not read any from these 34 cards.
- I looked for a 7-day-range or 7-day-price-change threshold that separates: none. Large
  7-day ranges run 6.10%–293.10%, calm 6.46%–88.19%; the medians (≈22% and ≈20%) are
  effectively the same.
